    About¶

    Platform architecture for regulated science, telecom, and open infrastructure¶

    I am a platform architect and staff-level cloud engineer based in Cambridge, UK. My work focuses on infrastructure that has to be secure, explainable, automated, and operable over time.

    The common thread across my career is platform ownership under real constraints: sensitive research data, telecom performance requirements, private cloud operations, air-gapped environments, security controls, and delivery timelines that require both technical depth and judgment.

    What I Work On¶

    Secure research platforms¶

    At Wellcome Sanger Institute, I work on cloud platform architecture and trusted research environments for sensitive genomic research. This includes Kubernetes platforms on OpenStack, GCP-based services, platform security controls, workload modernisation, and delivery patterns that support compliance without blocking research teams.

    Private cloud and Kubernetes platforms¶

    I have built and operated platforms across OpenStack, Kubernetes, OpenShift, Rancher, Ceph, MAAS, Juju, and GCP. I care less about the individual tools than the operating model they create: who owns the platform, how change is reviewed, how failures are detected, how workloads are isolated, and how teams recover.

    Telco and edge cloud¶

    At Canonical, I worked across EMEA telco cloud delivery, supporting architecture, deployment, automation, customer engagement, and production handover. The work covered OpenStack, Kubernetes, MAAS, Juju, Ceph, MicroK8s, air-gapped environments, monitoring, and CIS-aligned hardening.

    Automation and reliability¶

    I build automation to reduce uncertainty. Good automation makes platform state visible, change reviewable, recovery repeatable, and operational risk easier to reason about. My automation background includes Terraform, Ansible, Python, Bash, Juju, MAAS, CI/CD, GitOps patterns, and deployment tooling for OpenStack and Kubernetes environments.

    How I Think About Architecture¶

    I start with the operating model before implementation details. The important questions are usually not "which tool should we use?" but:

    • What are the data boundaries?
    • Who can make changes, and how are those changes reviewed?
    • What does failure look like?
    • What should be automated, and what should remain explicit?
    • What evidence do we need for compliance, recovery, and debugging?
    • Which parts of the platform must be standardised so teams can move faster?

    This is why my portfolio leads with research infrastructure, security, reliability, and platform architecture rather than a long technology list. The tools matter, but they are only useful when they support a clear operating model.
