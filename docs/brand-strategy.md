# Brand Strategy

This page records the positioning decisions behind the portfolio, resume, and
LinkedIn rewrite.

## Strongest Positioning

**Primary positioning: Platform Architect**

This is stronger than leading with "Cloud Engineer" because it describes the
scope of the work: architecture, security, operating models, delivery, and
long-term platform ownership.

Recommended usage:

- Portfolio: **Platform Architect**
- Resume: **Platform Architect / Staff Cloud Engineer**
- LinkedIn: **Platform Architect | Staff Cloud Engineer | Kubernetes, OpenStack, Cloud Security & Research Infrastructure**
- Recruiter keyword coverage: Staff Platform Engineer, Principal Cloud Engineer,
  Infrastructure Architect, Kubernetes Platform Lead, Cloud Security Architect,
  Research Computing Platform Architect.

## Why Not Lead With Tools

Tool lists near the title can make senior engineers look smaller than their
actual scope. A staff-level profile should lead with:

- Problems solved
- Architecture scope
- Operating constraints
- Security and reliability requirements
- Business or research value
- Evidence of leadership and judgment

Tools still matter, but they belong in capability sections, experience details,
case studies, and proof points.

## Current Portfolio Weaknesses Addressed

- The old homepage was too tool-led and too promotional.
- Several pages used casual or sales-heavy language that weakened seniority.
- Skills were presented as a broad inventory rather than coherent platform
  capabilities.
- Career pages read like personal stories rather than architecture summaries.
- Blog positioning was generic and did not support the platform architect brand.
- Recruiters could infer the target role, but it was not explicit enough.

## Information Architecture

Recommended public navigation:

1. Home
2. About
3. Experience
4. Capabilities
5. Resume
6. Certifications
7. Writing
8. Professional Materials
9. Contact

This structure gives different audiences a direct path:

- Recruiters: Home, Resume, Experience
- Engineering leaders: Home, About, Capabilities, Experience
- Technical peers: Writing, Capabilities, Experience
- Professional network: LinkedIn copy, achievements, contact

## Visual Hierarchy

The homepage should communicate in this order:

1. Positioning: Platform Architect / Staff-level Cloud Engineer.
2. Scope: secure research infrastructure, Kubernetes, OpenStack, cloud security,
   automation.
3. Evidence: TRE, EMEA telco cloud, 15,000+ lines OpenStack automation, 8+ years.
4. Work samples: Wellcome Sanger Institute, Canonical, xFlow Research.
5. Operating philosophy: security, reliability, automation, ownership.
6. CTA: resume, experience, contact.

## Tone Guide

Use:

- "platform architecture"
- "operating model"
- "trusted research environments"
- "secure delivery patterns"
- "reproducible infrastructure"
- "auditable automation"
- "operational reliability"

Avoid overusing:

- "visionary"
- "magic"
- "unstoppable"
- "bulletproof"
- "transformation toolkit"
- "next-generation" unless the context is specifically telco/NFV.

## External Inspiration

The site should borrow principles, not wording, from senior technical leaders:

- Kelsey Hightower: clear explanations and practical credibility.
- Charity Majors: production operations, reliability, and strong technical
  judgment.
- Liz Rice: security and systems explained concretely.
- Joe Beda: Kubernetes and platform fundamentals.
- Cornelia Davis: cloud-native architecture and organisational context.
- Google, Cloudflare, Stripe, Datadog, HashiCorp, Red Hat: restrained design,
  clear technical scope, and evidence-led credibility.
